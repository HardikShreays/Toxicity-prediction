\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
This study compared fingerprint-based classical models with GCN, MPNN, AttentiveFP, and
three hybrid approaches for 12 Tox21 endpoints. All models were evaluated using the same
splits and masked endpoint-wise metrics. The graph models performed competitively on
scaffold splits, but the fingerprint-plus-descriptor random forest remained the strongest
single model. Probability stacking was the most successful hybrid: it improved on its
parents on the balanced scaffold splits and was not statistically distinguishable from
the random-forest baseline in the exploratory paired test. End-to-end fusion did not
provide a benefit under the tested training setup.

The results support a measured conclusion: graph-learned representations are useful
complements to engineered chemical features, but they do not automatically outperform
strong fingerprint baselines. Future work should prioritize better-calibrated stacking,
regularization and tuning for end-to-end fusion, and evaluation on external or prospective
data.
